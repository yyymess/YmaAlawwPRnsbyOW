## 1. Steve Blank, "The Elves Leave Middle Earth – Soda's Are No Longer Free" (Dec 21, 2009)

- **Where/when:** steveblank.com, Dec 21, 2009. Picked up by Techmeme the same day; resurfaces on Hacker News repeatedly (2009, 2016, 2022).
- **Setup:** a ~3.5-year-old startup has passed 50 employees, hit cash-flow breakeven, raised a follow-on round, and just hired its first **CFO**.
- **The trigger:** the CFO's board deck covers the expected stuff (Sarbanes-Oxley compliance, a new accounting system, security) and then asks how much the company spends on **free sodas and snacks**, with a spreadsheet. To save roughly **$10,000**, the board starts charging for soda.
- **The symbol:** engineers find a sign on the machine: **"Soda now 50 cents."** Uproar.
- **The other changes that piled on:** the informal dinner reimbursement became formal **expense reports**; **"professional" managers** were hired over the engineering teams, often with more stock than the early engineers.
- **The argument:** the soda is only the wake-up call. Builders heads-down on product never noticed the company had become something different from what they joined. Each finance decision looks rational; together they trigger an **exodus of the best engineers**, who "can work literally anywhere" and will leave if they can do what they want elsewhere.
- **The metaphor:** early employees = Tolkien's **elves**, who sail from Middle-earth (the Grey Havens) when their age ends. The startup era is over; the "company" era of process people begins. Blank frames it as a natural transition, not pure villainy: startups search for a business model, companies execute one, and they need different people.
- **Short quotes (≤15 words, via search summaries):** "Soda now 50 cents" (the sign); "Do you know how much our company is spending on free sodas and snacks?" (CFO, ~14 words); best engineers "can work literally anywhere".
- **Sources:** https://steveblank.com/2009/12/21/the-elves-leave-middle-earth-%E2%80%93-soda%E2%80%99s-are-no-longer-free/ ; https://www.techmeme.com/091221/p75 ; https://readwrite.com/when-the-soda-stops-being-free/ ; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=31573108
- **[UNVERIFIED]:** the exact wording of the startup-vs-company passage, and whether Blank names the company (he does not appear to).
- **Visual:** a glowing soda fridge with a fresh coin slot and a taped note "Soda now 50 cents"; behind it, a line of pointy-eared engineers with laptops boarding a little white ship.

## 2. Ed Zitron, "The Men Who Killed Google" / "The Man Who Killed Google Search" (Apr 23/24, 2024)

- **What it is:** one newsletter post, not two. It lives at **https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-men-who-killed-google/** and is widely cited as **"The Man Who Killed Google Search."** Published around **April 23–24, 2024** (Boing Boing covered it Apr 23, Daring Fireball Apr 24).
- **"Men" vs "Man":** the URL slug and some reposts (e.g. a Blind thread titled "The Men Who Killed Google Search") use the plural. The headline as usually cited (Wikipedia, press) is singular. The plural fits the content: Prabhakar Raghavan is the main target, but the post also blames **Sundar Pichai** (who backed him) and ads leader **Jerry Dischler**. **[UNVERIFIED]:** whether the headline was retitled from plural to singular, or the slug just differs.
- **The argument in brief:**
  - In **Feb 2019**, Google's ads and finance side called a **"code yellow"** on Search because query growth and search revenue were lagging.
  - Head of Search **Ben Gomes** resisted making Search worse to pump queries. Raghavan, then head of Ads (and earlier Yahoo's head of search 2005–2012, during its decline), pushed for growth.
  - Zitron links this to 2019 changes: a March 2019 core update that he says rolled back quality improvements, and a May 2019 ad redesign that made ads look more like organic results.
  - In **2020** Raghavan replaced Gomes as head of Search. Zitron calls him a "managerial type" who put shareholder growth and "queries with commercial intent" ahead of product quality, the "Rot Economy" thesis.
  - Postscript (verified): on **Oct 17, 2024** Pichai announced Raghavan was leaving the Knowledge & Information (Search/Ads) job to become **"Chief Technologist, Google"**. **Nick Fox** took over Search and Ads. Pichai's memo said he would "return to his computer science roots." Press (Fortune: "Google's search and ads chief is out") and Zitron read it as a sideways exit. Sources: https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/17/google-replaces-executive-in-charge-of-search-and-advertising/ ; https://www.seroundtable.com/nick-fox-head-google-search-38263.html
- **Why it matters for the video:** it is the 2024 heir to Blank's essay. A finance/ads org takes over the engineering org that built the product, using an internal email chain as evidence (from the US v. Google antitrust trial exhibits).
- **Sources:** https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-men-who-killed-google/ ; https://boingboing.net/2024/04/23/how-one-power-hungry-leader-destroyed-google-search.html ; https://daringfireball.net/linked/2024/04/24/zitron-google-search ; https://www.seroundtable.com/how-man-killed-google-search-report-37281.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prabhakar_Raghavan ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Zitron
- **Visual:** a "CODE YELLOW" hazard-tape banner across a Google-ish search box; the blue results slowly turn into "Sponsored" labels; a suit hands a quarterly-revenue chart to a man in a hoodie who quietly walks off-frame.

## 3. Meta / Facebook: from "The Hacker Way" to "Year of Efficiency"

### The paradise era
- **Bootcamp (2008–):** six-week onboarding that every new engineer goes through. Bootcampers fix bugs across the whole codebase, then **choose their own team** at the end. Andrew "Boz" Bosworth started it in 2008, when engineering was nearing ~150 people, to keep the culture intact as the company scaled (described in a Nov 19, 2009 Facebook Engineering post). Source: https://engineering.fb.com/2009/11/19/production-engineering/facebook-engineering-bootcamp/
- **Like button born at a hackathon (2007):** PM Leah Pearlman and others proposed it. Engineer Justin Rosenstein built the prototype, then called the **"Awesome Button,"** during an all-night hackathon (around July 2007). Zuckerberg held it back for about two years, so insiders called it a "cursed project." It shipped in **Feb 2009**. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Like_button ; https://www.theringer.com/2017/02/15/tech/how-the-like-button-took-over-the-internet-ebe778be2459 ; https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/justinesharrock/zuckerberg-didnt-like-the-like-button-at-first-but-now-hes-b
- **1 Hacker Way (Dec 2011):** Facebook moved to the former **Sun Microsystems** campus in Menlo Park (57 acres) and gave it the vanity address **1 Hacker Way**. The central plaza is **"Hacker Square,"** with the word **"HACK"** set into the paving in letters big enough to read from the air (on satellite images). The famous blue **thumbs-up "Like" sign** at the entrance was swapped for the Meta infinity logo on **Oct 28, 2021**. The address is still 1 Hacker Way (one search summary claimed it became "1 Meta Way"; **[UNVERIFIED / likely wrong]**). Sources: https://sfist.com/2011/12/06/facebook_has_new_worrisome_vanity_s/ ; https://candor.co/articles/tech-careers/an-inside-look-at-facebook-s-headquarters ; https://www.paloaltoonline.com/news/2021/10/29/facebook-changes-its-name-to-meta/
- **Analog Research Laboratory (founded ~2010, Ben Barry and Everett Katigbak):** an in-house screen-printing shop making motivational "propaganda" posters. The first poster printed was **"Done is better than perfect,"** followed by **"Move fast and break things"** and **"What would you do if you weren't afraid?"** Others include "Stay focused and keep shipping" and "Fortune favors the bold" **[these last two from memory; UNVERIFIED]**. Sources: https://v1.benbarry.com/project/facebook-posters ; https://techcrunch.com/2012/07/25/facebook-designers-ask-what-would-you-do-if-you-werent-afraid/ ; https://www.fastcompany.com/90308216/want-a-glimpse-of-facebooks-soul-in-2019-look-at-its-internal-propaganda-lab
- **The Little Red Book (2012):** a 148-page red booklet by Ben Barry, given to employees around the time Facebook passed 1 billion users and IPO'd. The cover reads **"Facebook was not originally created to be a company."** It continues that Facebook was built for a social mission. (A free PDF was circulated in 2024.) Sources: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20241203-facebooks-little-red-book/ ; https://mattrickard.com/facebook-culture-book ; https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/28453676-facebook-was-not-originally-created-to-be-a-company
- **"The Hacker Way" IPO letter (S-1, Feb 1, 2012):** Zuckerberg told investors that Facebook has "a unique culture and management approach" called the Hacker Way. Key lines: "Done is better than perfect" is painted on the walls; **"Code wins arguments"**; hacking means building quickly and testing boundaries; hackathons. Sources: https://money.cnn.com/2012/02/01/technology/zuckerberg_ipo_letter/index.htm ; https://techcrunch.com/2012/02/01/facebook-ipo-letter/
- **Motto change (F8, Apr 30, 2014):** "Move fast and break things" became **"Move fast with stable infrastructure"** ("Move Fast With Stable Infra" on the slide). Zuckerberg said fixing what broke was costing more time than the speed bought. It came alongside a 2-year API stability guarantee. Sources: https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/30/facebook-api-guarantee/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_F8 ; Wikimedia photo of the slide: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mark_Zuckerberg_-_Move_Fast_with_Stable_Infra.jpg

## 4. Microsoft: private offices, then the knife fight, then the "growth mindset," then layoffs and AI

### Gates-era engineering culture (1975–2000)
- **Private offices:** the 1986 Redmond campus used **X-shaped buildings** to maximize windows, and **every programmer got a private office with a door** instead of a cubicle. The campus was meant as a distraction-free place to "sit and think" (Gates' phrase, per encyclopedia.com). The design drew on IBM's Santa Teresa (Silicon Valley) Lab and productivity research. Joel Spolsky (Excel PM, 1991–94) later built Fog Creek around the same principle. Sources: https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/microsoft-corp ; https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2006/09/07/a-field-guide-to-developers-2/ ; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6409287 ; https://carloarg02.medium.com/1998-memories-from-working-at-microsoft-in-the-nineties-b4de15cebc07
- **"Developers, developers, developers…":** Ballmer's sweaty chant, plus the separate "monkey dance" clip (Microsoft 25th-anniversary event, Sept 2000). The developers chant is usually dated **~2000** (one source says 1999). **[UNVERIFIED: exact event/date]** It became an iconic meme, and Ballmer repeated it at Microsoft's 50th-anniversary event in Apr 2025. Sources: https://blog.codinghorror.com/steve-ballmer-sweatiest-billionaire-ever/ ; https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/steve-ballmer-monkey-dance ; https://x.com/WindowsCentral/status/1908216518019576141

### Ballmer era (CEO Jan 2000 – Feb 2014): the "lost decade"
- **Stack ranking / "vitality curve":** managers had to sort each unit onto a forced **bell curve** (top/good/average/below/poor), so some share always got bottom ratings: no bonus, or out the door. Engineers avoided working beside other stars to protect their own rank.
- **Kurt Eichenwald, "Microsoft's Lost Decade," Vanity Fair, Aug 2012 issue (online early July 2012):** said every current and former employee he interviewed named stack ranking as the most destructive process inside the company. (Short quote: stack ranking was "the most destructive process inside of Microsoft".) The piece also covers missing e-books, search, social and mobile, and bureaucracy beating engineering. Sources: https://athena.ecs.csus.edu/~buckley/CSc191/Vanity_Fair_Microsofts_Lost_Decade_Aug_2012.pdf ; https://hbr.org/2012/08/how-microsoft-and-netflix-lost
- **Stack ranking abolished:** HR chief **Lisa Brummel's** email of **Nov 12, 2013** announced "no more curve," "no more rankings." Yahoo (Marissa Mayer) was adopting the same system at the same time. Sources: https://money.cnn.com/2013/11/13/technology/enterprise/microsoft-stack-ranking/index.html ; https://www.forbes.com/sites/petercohan/2013/11/13/marissas-second-epic-fail-microsoft-abolishes-stack-ranking-as-yahoo-ramps-it-up/
- **Manu Cornet's "Organizational Charts" cartoon (2011):** Cornet, then a Google engineer, drew six tech org charts. **Microsoft's divisions all point guns at each other** (Amazon is a strict pyramid, Google a tangled web, Facebook a mesh, Apple everyone wired to one dot, Oracle mostly lawyers). Nadella reproduced it on page one of his memoir *Hit Refresh* (2017) as a reason to change the culture. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manu_Cornet ; https://x.com/alexxubyte/status/1570778557957439489
- **Windows Vista:** "Longhorn" was so behind that **Jim Allchin** wrote in Jan 2004 that "LH is a pig," and in **Aug 2004** development was **reset** from scratch. Vista shipped to consumers **Jan 30, 2007**, late and widely disliked. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Windows_Vista ; https://longhorn.ms/the-reset/
- **Missed mobile:** in **Jan 2007**, Ballmer laughed at the iPhone ("$500… most expensive phone in the world," no keyboard). Microsoft bought Nokia's phone unit (~$7.2B, 2013–14), then **wrote off $7.6B and cut 7,800 jobs in July 2015**. Windows Phone held ~2.7% share in Q1 2015. Sources: https://techcrunch.com/2007/01/18/balmer-laughs-at-the-iphone-lies-completely/amp/ ; https://techcrunch.com/2015/07/08/microsoft-writes-down-7-6b-of-its-nokia-acquisition-announces-7800-layoffs ; https://www.seattletimes.com/business/microsoft/microsoft-to-cut-7800-jobs-as-it-restructures-phone-business/

### Nadella turnaround (CEO Feb 4, 2014–)
- **"Growth mindset":** borrowed from Carol Dweck's *Mindset* (his wife gave him the book). The pitch was to go from **"know-it-alls" to "learn-it-alls."** Laid out in *Hit Refresh* (2017). Cloud and AI (OpenAI partnership) turned Microsoft into a multi-trillion-dollar company. Sources: https://nextbigideaclub.com/magazine/conversation-microsofts-ceo-on-the-power-of-being-a-learn-it-all/17851/ ; https://store.hbr.org/product/satya-nadella-at-microsoft-instilling-a-growth-mindset/LBS128
- **Counterpoint:** Ed Zitron, "The Cult of Microsoft" (wheresyoured.at, early 2025) argues "growth mindset" turned into a corporate loyalty language used to justify pushing AI and cutting staff. **[date approx.; UNVERIFIED]** https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-cult-of-microsoft/

## 5. Quick hits

### Amazon: "Day 1" vs "Day 2"
- **Bezos, 2016 shareholder letter (published Apr 2017):** "Day 2 is stasis. Followed by irrelevance." It goes on to "excruciating, painful decline" and then death, "and that is why it is always Day 1." His four defenses: customer obsession, resisting proxies (process as proxy), embracing external trends, high-velocity decisions. Source: https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2016-letter-to-shareholders
- **PIP machinery:** Amazon aims for about **6% "unregretted attrition" (URA)** of office staff per year. It runs a two-stage PIP: **"Focus"** (secret) then **"Pivot"** (formal); documents say only ~5% of people who attempt Pivot complete it. Focus placements went from <2,000/month (Apr 2022) to 3,300+/month as layoffs began. Sources: https://www.seattletimes.com/business/amazon/internal-amazon-documents-shed-light-on-how-company-pressures-out-6-of-office-workers/ ; https://finance.yahoo.com/news/amazon-sharply-upped-performance-improvement-201337948.html
- **RTO:** Jassy memo of Sept 16, 2024 required **5 days/week in office from Jan 2, 2025**, ending the 3-day hybrid policy of May 2023 and "return to being in the office the way we were" pre-COVID. The same memo promised to cut managers ("flatten," +15% IC-to-manager ratio by Q1 2025) and a "bureaucracy mailbox" **[the last two details from memory; UNVERIFIED]**. Sources: https://www.geekwire.com/2024/amazon-will-return-to-being-in-the-office-the-way-we-were-before-the-pandemic-ceo-tells-employees/ ; https://www.shrm.org/topics-tools/news/amazon-to-require-workers-back-to-the-office-full-time

### Boeing: engineers to finance
- **Merger:** Boeing–McDonnell Douglas closed **Aug 1, 1997**. Boeing was the buyer, but MDC executives (Harry Stonecipher; also John McDonnell as the largest shareholder) came to dominate. The quip: "McDonnell Douglas bought Boeing with Boeing's money." **[common quip, unattributed]**
- **Stonecipher (COO 1997, CEO Dec 2003 – Mar 2005):** "...so that it is run like a business rather than a great engineering firm." Widely quoted, often attributed to a 2004 Chicago Tribune interview, but **not traced to a verified dated source** (per Wikipedia). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Stonecipher
- **HQ move (announced Mar 2001):** CEO Phil Condit moved headquarters from Seattle to **Chicago**, away from the engineers and factories in Renton/Everett, partly for ~$60M in tax incentives. ### HP: "The HP Way"
- Hewlett and Packard (founded 1939) ran a decentralized, engineer-trusting culture: management by walking around, open lab stock rooms, no layoffs where avoidable **[details from memory; UNVERIFIED]**. By the 1990s it had become bureaucratic.
- **Carly Fiorina (CEO 1999–Feb 2005):** Compaq merger (2002), ~30k layoffs, morale collapse. **Mark Hurd (2005–2010):** cut 15,200 (10%) right away, shrank IT from 19k to 8k and data centers from 85 to 6. Profits rose, but critics said the HP Way was "obliterated." Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Hurd ; https://www.sfgate.com/news/article/HP-taps-operational-genius-to-lead-the-way-2719382.php ; https://www.forbes.com/2005/05/18/0518autofacescan01.html
- **Visual:** the HP garage at 367 Addison Ave with a "FOR LEASE — OPEX REDUCTION" sign.
