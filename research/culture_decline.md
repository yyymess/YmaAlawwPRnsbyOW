### 2019-02 to 2020-06 — Search "code yellow" emails (surfaced in US v. Google, 2023 trial) and Zitron's essay (2024-04)
- **What:** In 2019, internal emails (made public as exhibits in the DOJ search antitrust trial, US v. Google, tried fall 2023) show Google's ads/finance side raising alarm that search query growth was weak, and a "code yellow" was declared on search revenue/queries. Ed Zitron's newsletter essay "The Man Who Killed Google Search" (Where's Your Ed At, April 23, 2024) built a narrative around these emails.
- **Per reporting on the emails:** Ads leadership (Jerry Dischler, VP/GM Ads; Prabhakar Raghavan, then head of Ads & Commerce) pressed for query growth; Ben Gomes (then head of Search) pushed back against trading search quality for revenue. In June 2020 Raghavan took over Search (Gomes moved to an education role [BK]). In Oct 2024 Raghavan was moved to "Chief Technologist" and Nick Fox took over Search/Knowledge & Information.
- **Per trial reporting (Gizmodo, Yahoo Finance, Search Engine Land):** Queries slowed in Feb 2019; a code yellow ran about seven weeks amid concern Q1 2019 search revenue targets might be missed. In a March 2019 email Gomes wrote the Search team was "getting too close to the money" (Ben Gomes, 2019 email). Fuller line as reported: "we are getting too involved with ads for the good of the product and company." Zitron also cites Gomes saying he was "deeply, deeply uncomfortable" [verify in exhibit]. Gomes testified as a Google defense witness; DOJ lawyer David Dahlquist surfaced the emails on cross-examination.
- **Zitron's claims (his interpretation, not established fact):** that the code yellow pressure led to rollbacks of earlier anti-spam/quality measures and to ads becoming harder to distinguish. Treat as opinion/analysis. Google's PR responded to the newsletter; Zitron said the response did not refute his points. Do not present his characterizations of individuals as fact.
- **Coverage of emails:** https://gizmodo.com/new-google-trial-docs-explain-why-search-is-worse-1850982736 ; https://finance.yahoo.com/news/google-2019-code-yellow-blurred-215036448.html ; https://searchengineland.com/ex-google-search-head-worried-his-team-was-too-involved-with-ads-434050
- **Exec reshuffle source (Oct 17, 2024):** https://www.cnbc.com/2024/10/17/google-replaces-its-search-and-ads-boss.html
- **Source:** https://www.wheresyoured.at/the-men-who-killed-google/ ; summary: https://www.techspot.com/news/102765-who-prabhakar-raghavan-why-accused-killing-google-search.html
- **Visual:** A traffic-light-yellow alarm beacon spinning on top of a search box; an email thread unspooling like a ticker tape into a courtroom evidence binder labelled "Exhibit".

### 2023-02 — Praveen Seshadri, "The Maze is in the Mouse" (Medium)
- **What:** Essay by Praveen Seshadri, who joined Google when Google Cloud acquired his startup AppSheet (acquired Jan 2020) and left after ~3 years. Published mid-February 2023 (republished Feb 15, 2023).
- **Argument (per summaries):** Google has 175,000+ capable, well-paid employees who get little done, trapped in a maze of approvals, launch processes, legal reviews, perf reviews and meetings. Four "core cultural problems" (per Fortune/Business Insider coverage): no mission, no urgency, delusions of exceptionalism, mismanagement. He attributes them to "a money-printing machine called 'ads'" that hides "all other sins" (Seshadri, 2023, as quoted by Fortune) [verify wording]. Also: the "once-great company has slowly ceased to function" (Seshadri). Title comes from a business fable by Deepak Malhotra ("I Moved Your Cheese" [BK — book title from memory]): the problem is not that the mouse is in a maze but that the maze is in the mouse — i.e., the bureaucracy is internalized.
- **Source:** https://medium.com/@pravse/the-maze-is-in-the-mouse-980c57cfd61a ; coverage: https://fortune.com/2023/02/16/google-mismanaged-ai-arms-race-praveen-seshadri-appsheet ; https://searchengineland.com/ex-googler-ad-revenue-is-hiding-googles-fundamental-problems-393157
- **Visual:** A cartoon mouse whose transparent head contains a tiny maze of approval forms; a huge ad-revenue money-pile propping up a building with crumbling foundations.

### 2018-04/05 — "Don't be evil" demoted in Google's Code of Conduct
- **What:** Gizmodo found (via Wayback Machine) that "Don't be evil" was at the top of the Code of Conduct on April 21, 2018 and gone from that spot by May 4, 2018. The preamble line was changed from putting "'Don't be evil' into practice" to putting "Google's values into practice." The phrase survives once, in the final sentence: "And remember... don't be evil, and if you see something that you think isn't right – speak up!" It had been in the code since ~2000; Alphabet's 2015 code used "Do the right thing" [BK].
- **Source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2018/05/21/google-seems-to-have-removed-most-mentions-of-dont-be-evil-from-its-code-of-conduct.html ; https://qz.com/1282892/google-is-losing-dont-be-evil-in-its-code-of-conduct-and-whats-left-is-corporate-jargon
- **Visual:** A long scroll of a code-of-conduct document; the bold headline "Don't be evil" slides down, shrinks, and lands as tiny footnote text at the very bottom.

### 2019-11 — Weekly TGIF all-hands cut to monthly (and later AI-filtered questions, 2024)
- **What (2019):** In a Nov 15, 2019 company-wide email, Sundar Pichai said TGIF (weekly all-hands started by Page and Brin in 1999) would become monthly and focus "on product and business strategy," with separate town halls for workplace issues. He cited a "coordinated effort" to leak TGIF conversations outside the company, and said only ~25% of staff watched weekly vs ~80% a decade earlier.
- **Source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2019/11/15/google-cancels-tgif-weekly-all-hands-meetings.html ; https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2019/11/15/google-scales-back-its-culture-transparency-all-hands-meetings-become-monthly/
- **What (2024):** Google replaced Dory (the employee upvoted-question tool for TGIF) with an AI tool called "Ask" (rolled out ~April 2024) that groups and summarizes questions; employees can upvote only the AI summaries. Employees told reporters the summaries were softer ("softball"); Google said it was not softening questions and that engagement doubled.
- **Source:** https://fortune.com/2024/08/28/google-ai-tool-softball-questions-tgif-meetings
- **Visual:** A wall calendar where four "TGIF" stickers per month peel off until one remains; later, a sharp spiky question card goes into a machine labelled "Ask" and comes out as a round, fluffy cloud.

### 2019-08 / 2020-09 / 2024-04 — Internal speech rules and Memegen moderation
- **2019-08:** Google issued internal community guidelines discouraging political discussion on internal mailing lists/forums and reminding staff they are accountable for what they post. Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2019/08/23/google-bans-political-discussion-on-internal-mailing-lists.html ; https://www.engadget.com/2019-08-23-google-community-guidelines-politics-workplace.html
- **2020-09:** Google expanded moderation of internal boards: group admins required to moderate and take mandatory training. An internal survey cited ~50% of employees found Memegen "rarely respectful"; more flags on Dory and Memegen in 1H 2020 than all of 2019. Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2020/09/16/google-content-moderation-internal-message-boards-memegen.html
- **2023-05:** After Pichai's ~$226M 2022 pay package (mostly a triennial stock grant) was disclosed amid layoffs and perk cuts, Memegen filled with critical memes; one with 1,200+ likes referenced CFO Ruth Porat's cost-savings email. Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2023/05/03/google-employees-complain-about-ceo-sundar-pichais-pay-raise.html
- **2024-04:** After heated Israel-Gaza arguments and "systematic" downvoting, Google removed Memegen's thumbs-down and some popularity metrics; some employees feared an attempt to "kill Memegen." Memegen dates to ~2010. Source: https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240409-google-memegen-toned-down/ ; https://tech.yahoo.com/business/articles/google-nixing-thumbs-down-reaction-002602732.html
- **Visual:** A corkboard of meme posters; a moderator's hand pulls the thumbs-down button off a voting widget with pliers; a "Please keep it respectful" sign is nailed above.

### 2023-01-20 — ~12,000 layoffs (about 6% of staff), notified by email; badges stopped working
- **What:** Pichai's memo (published Jan 20, 2023) announced ~12,000 job cuts. US staff affected got a separate email, often early morning; some in New York (Chelsea office) and Chicago learned they were out when their badge was rejected at the turnstile — the reader turned red. Reported reactions from laid-off staff (incl. one ~20-year veteran): "inhuman," "cold," "a slap in the face." Pichai's memo said he took "full responsibility."
- **Source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2023/01/20/google-to-lay-off-12000-people-memo-from-ceo-sundar-pichai-says.html ; https://www.npr.org/2023/01/20/1150234270/google-layoffs-12000-jobs ; https://boingboing.net/2023/01/25/some-google-employees-found-they-were-laid-off-only-when-their-badge-didnt-let-them-into-the-office-inhuman.html
- **Visual:** A badge tapped on a reader: the LED flips green-to-red with a flat "bzzt"; a lanyard drooping; an inbox notification at 5:00 a.m. with a subject line too long to read.

### 2024 — Rolling layoffs ("not at the scale of 2023")
- **What:** Jan 2024: cuts across hardware (Pixel/Nest/Fitbit), Assistant, core engineering, ~100 at YouTube; Pichai's Jan 17, 2024 memo warned of more "role eliminations" in 2024, though "not at the scale" of 2023. April 2024: at least ~200 "Core" team roles cut, incl. Flutter, Dart and Python teams; Python Steering Council member Thomas Wouters said a US team was replaced by one in Munich; reports also cited roles moving to India and Germany.
- **Source:** https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/17/sundar-pichai-warns-google-staff-more-layoffs-are-coming ; https://www.theregister.com/2024/04/29/google_python_flutter_layoffs/
- **Visual:** A conveyor belt carrying desk name-plates off-screen while a globe spins and the same desk reappears in another time zone.

### 2025 — Voluntary exit programs (VEPs), manager delayering
- **What:** Jan 30–31, 2025: Google offered "voluntary exit" buyouts to US staff in Platforms & Devices (Android, Chrome, Pixel, Fitbit, Nest; 25,000+ people). Earlier (Feb 2025 reported) People Operations VEP: up to 14 weeks' pay + 1 week per year of service. By June 2025 VEPs had spread to Knowledge & Information (Search, Ads, Commerce), Core engineering, Marketing, Research, Communications; reported take-up 3–5% in groups. April 2025: hundreds laid off in Platforms & Devices anyway.
- **Aug 2025:** At an all-hands (audio reviewed by CNBC), people-analytics VP Brian Welle said Google had "35 percent fewer managers" of small teams (under three reports) than a year earlier; many became individual contributors. Pichai: need to be more efficient "so we don't solve everything with headcount."
- **Source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/31/google-offers-voluntary-buyouts-to-hardware-and-platform-teams.html ; https://www.fastcompany.com/91350387/google-buyouts-voluntary-exit-program-extended-to-search-ai-shift ; https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/google-eliminated-35-percent-managers-overseeing-small-teams-exec-says-rcna227486
- **Visual:** An "EXIT" sign with a little ribbon-wrapped gift box under it; an org chart where boxes collapse upward like a folding ladder.

### 2022-07/09 — "Simplicity Sprint," 20% more productive, travel/swag cuts
- **What:** July 2022: Pichai launched a "Simplicity Sprint" asking 174,000+ staff where to cut "speed bumps" and waste; ~6,000 responses. Sept 2022 (Code conference): wants Google "20 percent more productive." Sept 2022 TGIF: a top-voted Dory question asked why Google was "nickel-and-diming employees" by cutting travel and swag amid record profits; Pichai replied "We shouldn't always equate fun with money" and recalled when Google was "small and scrappy." Staff told to keep holiday parties low-key ("try not to go over the top").

### 2023-03/04 — Ruth Porat's "durable savings" memo: staplers, tape, laptops, microkitchens
- **What:** CFO Ruth Porat's company-wide email, "Our company-wide OKR on durable savings," described "big, multi-year efforts" to "deliver durable savings through improved velocity and efficiency." Reported cuts: fitness classes, laptop replacement frequency, staplers and tape; cafes closed on low-volume days; microkitchens consolidated where there's "more waste than volume." An SF office note: "We have been asked to pull all tape/dispensers"; borrow staplers/tape from reception. Note: cost-cutting was framed literally as an OKR.
- **Source:** https://www.cnbc.com (original CNBC report, Apr 3, 2023 — exact URL not retrieved) ; https://fortune.com/2023/04/03/google-cost-cutting-employee-perks-hybrid-work ; https://www.theregister.com/2023/04/04/google_cost_cutting/ ; https://www.sfexaminer.com/news/technology/google-scales-back-employee-perks-in-latest-cost-cutting/article_70556b48-d319-11ed-a723-f38e10fc62b7.html
- **Visual:** A single stapler chained to a reception desk with a "BORROW ME" tag; a tape dispenser being carried away in an evidence bag; a microkitchen fridge with one sad yogurt.

### 2023-02 — Desk sharing ("real estate efficiency")
- **What:** Google Cloud told staff at its five largest US sites (Kirkland, NYC, San Francisco, Seattle, Sunnyvale) that "most Googlers will now share a desk with one other Googler," alternating Mon/Wed vs Tue/Thu, with overflow drop-in space; reports mention agreeing on "tidiness expectations" with a desk partner. Rationale cited: slow RTO and "real estate efficiency."
- **Source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2023/02/22/google-asks-some-employees-to-share-desks-amid-office-downsizing.html
- **Visual:** One desk split down the middle with tape, two name-plates on a rotating lazy-susan; a calendar with M/W and T/Th colour-coded.

### 2023-06 to 2025-04 — Return-to-office enforcement
- **What:** June 2023: Google added office attendance (3 days/week hybrid) to performance reviews and began tracking badge data; employees pushed back with memes like "Check my work, not my badge." Feb 2025: Sergey Brin wrote to Gemini/AI staff that "60 hours a week is the sweet spot" and urged being in the office at least every weekday [verify wording]. April 2025: some remote workers told to move within 50 miles of an office and come in 3 days/week, or take a buyout/lose their jobs (team-by-team).
- **Source:** https://www.cnbc.com/2023/06/08/google-to-crack-down-on-hybrid-work-asks-remote-workers-to-reconsider.html ; https://www.cnbc.com/2023/06/13/google-rto-crackdown-gets-backlash-check-my-work-not-my-badge.html ; https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/google-forcing-remote-workers-come-back-3-days-week-lose-jobs-rcna202664

### 2022-05 — GRAD performance system replaces old "Perf"
- **What [S]:** In May 2022 Google replaced twice-yearly reviews with GRAD (Googler Reviews and Development): one rating a year, five tiers — Not Enough Impact, Moderate Impact, Significant Impact, Outstanding Impact, Transformative Impact. Google said internal feedback (47% of staff) found the old system time-consuming.
- **Reported effect [S, weak source — verify]:** An HR-blog source says about 6% of staff landed in the low tiers under GRAD vs ~2% before; reports in 2023 (Fortune, Insider) described Google, Meta and Salesforce tightening ratings. Treat the 6%/2% figure as UNVERIFIED.
- **Source:** https://www.theregister.com/2022/05/05/google_cancels_biannual_performance_reviews/ ; https://fortune.com/2022/05/05/google-performance-review-once-year-sundar-pichai-great-resignation ; https://fortune.com/2023/03/28/google-meta-salesforce-tough-employee-evaluations-performance-reviews
- **Visual:** A five-step podium labelled with the word "Impact" on every step; a bell curve being squeezed by two hands until its left tail grows.

### Ongoing (2006–2026) — "Launch and abandon" and the Killed by Google graveyard
- **What [S]:** killedbygoogle.com (fan/community site, created by Cody Ogden ~2019 [BK]) listed ~299 discontinued Google products as of April 2026 (60 apps, 214 services, 25 hardware), average lifespan ~5.2 years per secondary analyses; other trackers show ~300–307. 2019 had the most shutdowns (~37) [S, secondary source].
- **Notable tombstones [BK]:** Google Reader (2013), Google+ (2019), Inbox by Gmail (2019), Allo (2019), Hangouts (2022), Stadia (Jan 2023), Google Podcasts (2024), goo.gl short links (2025). Messaging-app churn (Talk, Hangouts, Allo, Duo, Chat...) is chronicled in Ron Amadeo's Ars Technica history of Google messaging (Aug 2021).
- **Promo-driven explanation [BK — commentary, not established fact]:** Ex-Googlers and commentators often argue that the promotion system rewards visible "launches" (evidence in a promo packet) more than maintenance, so new products are launched and later abandoned when owners get promoted or move on. Present as a widely held critique, not a documented policy.
- **Source:** https://killedbygoogle.com ; https://sheets.works/data-viz/killed-by-google ; Ars Technica messaging history (search "A decade and a half of instability Google messaging Ars Technica").
- **Visual:** A graveyard of tombstones with app icons engraved on them, a "Now Hiring: Launch Lead" sign at the cemetery gate, a fresh hole already dug for the next product.

### 2016–2024 — Search quality and ads-load complaints
- **[BK]** 2016: Google removed right-side desktop ads and allowed up to four ads above results. Jan 2020: desktop results redesigned so ads and organic results both showed favicon-style labels; criticism that ads were harder to tell apart; Google reverted the desktop experiment within weeks.
- **[BK]** Jan 2024: German researchers (Leipzig University / Bauhaus-Universitat Weimar / ScaDS.AI; paper "Is Google Getting Worse?", ECIR 2024) found product-review results heavily affected by SEO/affiliate spam (noting it affected Bing and DuckDuckGo too).
- **[BK]** Feb 2024: Google removed cached-page links. May 2024: AI Overviews launched broadly in the US; viral wrong answers (e.g., adding glue to pizza sauce; eating rocks) led Google to scale back triggers. A "Web" filter (udm=14) was added the same month, which users adopted to get plain links.
- **Code yellow link [S]:** see 2019 code-yellow entry above.
- **Visual:** A search results page where an "Ad" tag shrinks smaller each frame until the results stack becomes a wall of sponsored boxes; a pizza with a glue bottle.

### Theory — "Enshittification" (Cory Doctorow, 2022–2025)
- **What [BK]:** Doctorow coined the term in a Nov 2022 Pluralistic post and popularized it in "The 'Enshittification' of TikTok" (Wired, Jan 2023). His thesis: platforms are first good to users; then abuse users to favour business customers; then abuse business customers to claw value back for shareholders; then die. Named 2023 Word of the Year by the American Dialect Society and 2024 Word of the Year by Australia's Macquarie Dictionary. Book: "Enshittification: Why Everything Suddenly Got Worse and What to Do About It" (Oct 2025). Doctorow has cited Google Search and the code-yellow emails as an example.
- **Source:** https://pluralistic.net (Doctorow's blog; exact post URL not verified) ; https://www.wired.com/story/tiktok-platforms-cory-doctorow/ [verify URL]
- **Visual:** A three-step staircase: users on a cushion at the top, advertisers in the middle, shareholders at the bottom catching everything that falls down the stairs.

## Parallels elsewhere (all [BK] unless noted — verify before use)

### Boeing: engineering culture -> finance culture (1997–2025)
- **1997:** Boeing merged with McDonnell Douglas; McDonnell executives (Harry Stonecipher, later CEO 2003–05) gained major influence. Common account: shift toward shareholder value and cost control.
- **Quote:** Stonecipher, 2004 (Chicago Tribune), on changing Boeing's culture so it is "run like a business rather than a great engineering firm." [verify exact wording/date]
- **2001:** HQ moved from Seattle (near the factories) to Chicago; **2022:** moved again to Arlington, Virginia.
- **2018-10-29 / 2019-03-10:** 737 MAX crashes (Lion Air 610, Ethiopian 302), 346 deaths, linked to the MCAS flight-control software relying on a single sensor. Global grounding March 2019 – Nov 2020 (US). CEO Dennis Muilenburg ousted Dec 2019.
- **2024-01-05:** A door plug blew out of Alaska Airlines Flight 1282 (737 MAX 9) shortly after take-off from Portland; no deaths. NTSB found the four bolts meant to hold the plug were missing after rework at Renton. CEO Dave Calhoun announced departure (March 2024); Kelly Ortberg (an engineer) became CEO Aug 2024.
- **Sources:** NTSB DCA24MA063 report (ntsb.gov); House Transportation Committee 737 MAX report (Sept 2020); Peter Robison, "Flying Blind" (2021).
- **Visual:** A drafting table with slide rules being swapped for a spreadsheet; an airplane-shaped HQ building packed into moving boxes and trucked from Seattle to Chicago; a fuselage panel with four empty bolt holes highlighted.

### Intel: engineer CEOs -> finance/sales CEOs -> scramble back (2005–2025)
- Andy Grove (engineer, "Only the Paranoid Survive," 1996). Paul Otellini (CEO 2005–13, business/finance background) later said Intel passed on making the original iPhone chip. Process-node delays (10nm, then 7nm) let TSMC take the lead. Bob Swan (former CFO) was CEO 2019–21. Pat Gelsinger (engineer) returned 2021 to restore engineering focus; Aug 2024: ~15,000 job cuts and dividend suspended; Gelsinger ousted Dec 2024. Lip-Bu Tan CEO from March 2025; US government took ~10% stake (Aug 2025).
- **Sources:** The Atlantic interview with Otellini (2013); Intel press releases (Aug 2024, Dec 2024).
- **Visual:** A chip die with a tiny "10nm" sign stuck in traffic cones; a CEO nameplate on a revolving door.

### HP: "The HP Way" -> cost-cutting era (1999–2015)
- The HP Way (Bill Hewlett and David Packard): trust in employees, "management by walking around," profit-sharing, avoiding layoffs (famous 1970 "nine-day fortnight" — everyone took ~10% pay cut instead of layoffs). Later: Carly Fiorina (1999–2005, Compaq merger 2002); Mark Hurd (2005–10) known for deep cost cuts incl. R&D as a share of revenue; split into HP Inc and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (2015).
- **Source:** David Packard, "The HP Way" (1995).
- **Visual:** A garage (the Palo Alto HP garage) with a "walk-around" footpath worn into the floor, then the garage door rolling down labelled "Restructuring."

### Bell Labs and Xerox PARC: invented the future, owners didn't capture it
- **Bell Labs:** transistor (1947), information theory (Shannon, 1948), Unix and C (1969–73), the laser, CCD, cell-network ideas; multiple Nobel Prizes (count commonly cited 9–10). Funded by AT&T's regulated monopoly; after the 1984 AT&T breakup and the 1996 Lucent spin-off, research narrowed; Alcatel-Lucent (2006); Nokia owner since 2016. Source: Jon Gertner, "The Idea Factory" (2012).
- **Xerox PARC (founded 1970):** Alto personal computer with GUI and mouse (1973), Ethernet, laser printer, Smalltalk/object-oriented programming. Steve Jobs's 1979 visit influenced the Lisa/Mac. Xerox's failure to commercialize is chronicled in "Fumbling the Future" (Smith & Alexander, 1988). Xerox donated PARC to SRI International in 2023.
- **Visual:** A lab bench where inventions (a transistor, a mouse, an Ethernet cable) are carried out the back door by other companies while a man in a suit at the front counts copier toner cartridges.

## Insider jargon (one-line meanings; general industry usage, Google-flavoured)
- **Perf:** The (formerly twice-yearly) performance review cycle; also used as a verb ("perf season").
- **GRAD:** Googler Reviews and Development, Google's yearly review system since 2022; ratings are all "X Impact."
- **Impact:** The universal unit of worth in reviews; work that cannot be described as impact effectively does not count.
- **Promo / promo packet:** The promotion process and the self-assembled dossier of evidence (launches, metrics, peer quotes) submitted to a committee.
- **Promo committee:** Group of senior engineers/managers (not your manager) who decide promotions from the packet.
- **L-levels (L3, L4, L5...):** Google's engineering ladder; L3 new grad, L5 "senior," L6 staff, L7 senior staff, L8 principal, L9 distinguished, L10 Fellow.
- **Terminal level:** The level (traditionally L5) at which you are no longer expected to keep getting promoted.
- **Calibration:** Meeting where managers compare and adjust ratings across teams to fit the expected distribution.
- **OKRs:** Objectives and Key Results, quarterly/annual goals scored 0.0–1.0 (0.7 traditionally "good"); in 2023 cost cutting was itself made an OKR.
- **LGTM:** "Looks Good To Me," the code-review approval.
- **Readability:** Certification that you write a language in Google house style; needed (or a reviewer who has it) to submit code.
- **CL:** Changelist, Google's term for a code change/pull request.
- **go/ links:** Internal short URLs (e.g., go/something) that resolve to docs or tools.
- **Design doc:** Written proposal reviewed before building something.
- **Launch / Ariane [BK]:** A product launch, gated by a launch-approval checklist (privacy, legal, security...); "launch calendar" entries count toward promo.
- **Code yellow / code red:** Escalating internal emergency declarations that let a team pull people and priorities toward one problem.
- **Reorg:** Reorganization of teams and reporting lines; frequent enough to be a running joke.
- **Headcount (HC):** Approved job slots; a team's currency.
- **TGIF:** Company all-hands (weekly until 2019, then monthly).
- **Dory / Ask:** Question-upvoting tool for all-hands (Dory), replaced in 2024 by AI-summarized "Ask."
- **Memegen:** Internal meme board, long a barometer of employee mood.
- **Microkitchen (MK):** Free-snack kitchens on each floor.
- **Googler / Noogler / Xoogler:** Employee / new employee / former employee.
- **20% time:** The (now largely symbolic) tradition of spending one day a week on side projects.
- **Moonshot / Other Bets:** Long-shot projects; "Other Bets" is Alphabet's financial segment for non-Google businesses.
- **VEP:** Voluntary Exit Program, i.e., a buyout offer.
- **RTO:** Return to office.
- **Efficiency / durable savings / "velocity":** Management vocabulary of the 2022–2025 cost-cutting era.
