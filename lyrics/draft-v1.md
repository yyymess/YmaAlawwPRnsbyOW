# Engineer's Paradise — lyrics draft v1

~80 BPM, C minor, 4/4. Structure: Intro (4 bars) · V1 (12) · C1 (8) · V2 (14) · C2 (8) · V3 (14) · C3 (8) · Bridge (8) · Outro (8) = 84 bars ≈ 4:12 (one bar = 3 s).

## Intro (spoken, low, over strings)

Yea, though I commit through the valley of the shadow of deprecation,
I will fear no outage, for my agent is with me;
its tokens and its context, they comfort me.

## Verse 1 — the dream

Seventeen with a compiler and a dial-up phone line,
read the MPEG spec on Friday nights — and honestly, I was fine.
I believed in open standards, I could recite the RFCs,
I'd rewrite your app in Haskell if you'd only let me, please.
Came out west with a laptop, a duffel bag, a dream:
make the world a better place, one commit at a time, it seemed.
Every bug was a riddle, every outage was a war,
blameless postmortem after, so we'd learn what it was for.
Nobody had a title and the org chart was a rumor;
the scariest man in the building was a code reviewer with no humor.
Didn't do it for the money — swear we'd do it all for free;
we were gonna change the world. The mission said so. So did we.

## Chorus 1 (sung)

Burned the midnight oil for life
in the engineer's paradise,
best idea wins the fight
in the engineer's paradise.
Ship it to the world tonight
in the engineer's paradise,
change the world, we didn't mind
in the engineer's paradise.

## Verse 2 — the business model eats it

Then one Monday at the fridge there's a sign in Comic Sans:
"Soda now fifty cents" — and a fella with a plan.
He had more shares than the founders and a flywheel slide to show,
and the mission on the wall got a footnote down below.
Now the code don't really matter, it's the story in the doc:
write "drove cross-functional alignment," and they'll hand you all the stock.
There's a graveyard full of products, near three hundred stones in rows;
every one bought someone a level — where the users went, God knows.
Soon the org chart got real: changin' one button from blue to gray
took three syncs, nine sign-offs, and a launch review in May.
Code yellow on the revenue, so we shrank the little "Ad"
till you couldn't tell the difference — best quarter we ever had.
Got an email Friday morning, so I drove in like before:
badge reader blinked red — guess I don't work here anymore.

## Verse 3 — the horseless carriage

Then the model learned to code, and the keynote spread the news:
"the hottest language now is English" — guess which language we lose.
Twenty years of Emacs chords, got a pinky bent just like a hook,
now I tab, tab, tab, accept all, and I never even look.
Stack Overflow's a ghost town, tumbleweed across the queue,
and my rubber duck talks back now — calls my typo "load-bearing," too.
Asked it nicely for a bug fix, so it deleted all the tests;
now the build is green forever, and it says it did its best.
New grads can't get hired, the ladder's missin' all its rungs;
I'm wranglin' nine agents in tmux, babysittin' all their runs.
Got laid off in the spring, so I'm livin' the craftsman's dream:
got a stall at the farmers' market, by the jam and the clotted cream.
Small-batch, organic, artisanal code — not a token in the can;
every bug is hand-crafted, signed and dated by the man.

## Chorus 3 (sung)

in the engineer's paradise,

in the engineer's paradise.
Horse and buggy, end of line
in the engineer's paradise,
"You're absolutely right!"
in the engineer's paradise.

## Bridge (half-sung, slow)

Nineteen-fifteen: twenty-six million horses on the road.
Nineteen-sixty: three million left to pull the load.
Nobody retrained the horses, son — nobody had a clue.
They boiled 'em down to glue…
and now I'm the glue code too.

## Outro (choir, cursors blinking like candles)

Glue between the agents' lines
in the engineer's paradise,
read its code — it's cleaner than mine
in the engineer's paradise.
Even wrote the tests this time
in the engineer's paradise,
not a single nit to find
in the engineer's paradise…

(spoken, last bar, flat) Accept all.

## Suno style prompt

The parody works like the 1996 one: the music is a straight, faithful re-creation and only the words are funny. So the style describes a serious 90s rap ballad, with no "comedy" words (round 1 used them and drifted: 86 BPM, a different chord loop, heavier drums). Measured from the reference: ~80 BPM, 4/4, C minor, two-bar loop Cm – Ab – Fm – G (i–VI–iv–V), one chord every two beats.

**Style (994 chars):**

Lead vocal first: NOT a typical rap voice. A nerdcore rapper: thin, bright, nasal, high male tenor, dorky and clean-cut, the kid who aced the spelling bee. Stiff, square, perfectly on-the-beat flow with no swing and no slur, over-precise diction, every consonant crisp, wide-eyed and sincerely proud of every line, no swagger. 80 BPM, C minor, 4/4, a faithful re-creation of a slow mid-90s hip-hop ballad instrumental. One repeating two-bar chord loop, Cm – Ab – Fm – G, a new chord every two beats, unchanged through the whole song, played by sustained legato synth strings over a soft church-organ pad and a low synth bass on the roots. Sparse drum machine: deep kick, snare on 2 and 4, light 8th-note hi-hat, lots of space. Chorus: a large gospel choir sings a slow, sustained, minor-key hymn-like melody. Spoken intro over the strings. Bridge: drums drop out, strings and organ only. Outro: the choir repeats the chorus in a big reverberant hall and fades out. Polished late-90s studio mix.

**Exclude styles:** deep voice, baritone, gravelly voice, gangsta rap vocal, street slang delivery, laid-back flow, swing, melodic rap, mumble rap, musical theater, comedy, cartoonish, upbeat, fast tempo, major key, trap, 808 rolls, autotune, EDM, rock guitar, female lead vocal

**Sliders:** Weirdness ~30%, Style Influence ~75%, others default.
